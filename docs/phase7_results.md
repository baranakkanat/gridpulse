# Phase 7 results: continuous model-residual correction versus binary alarm feedback

**Status.**

- **Protocol:** `docs/phase7_protocol.md`, with amendment 7A.
- **Evaluation:** one run on 2026-10-18 (2016-07-01 – 12-30, 183 days), after a freeze that
  bound it to the code, dependency pins and protocol of the amended development stage.
- **Hashes:** `docs/provenance/phase7/registration_log.txt`.
- **Post-evaluation changes:** the code changed after the evaluation, but no registered
  result did (s. 11). The registration log records the new hashes and the replay evidence.
- **Full tables:** `outputs/margin_study/logs/evaluation_report.md` (git-ignored),
  regenerated by `docs/provenance/phase7/scripts/report.py evaluation`.

**Identity.**

- This is an **exploratory replication**: the six SimBench LV networks, the 2016 profiles,
  the periods, the truths and the placements were examined in Phases 4–6.
- No unexamined SimBench LV network exists.
- The decision rules were set after a development probe, with the ORIGINAL Phase 5/6
  results known.
- **Nothing here is confirmatory, and nothing is evidence about a real feeder.**

**Reading guide.**

- Totals are over the five new networks.
- "Mismatch" covers the three model-error truths: transformer tap, MV voltage 1.055 p.u.,
  and PV at fixed cos φ 0.95. All three are constant and in the direction that raises
  voltage or loading.
- A *cell* is (network, truth). The requirement is at most 0.1 avoidable violation
  quarter-hours per day.
- **Normalised margins:** 0.01 = 0.001 p.u. of voltage = 1 percentage point of loading.
- **"Substation only" means** substation telemetry plus complete, exact 15-minute
  customer injection metering for the previous day. The residuals are computed against
  the nominal model at those injections (s. 8).

## 1. Answer in brief

1. **The registered primary hypothesis H1 is NOT met.**
   - Substation-only continuous correction (C1) has 1 mismatch violation against 513 for
     binary feedback with two exact remote meters (B3), so H1(a) holds. It pays for this
     with 1027 against 728 mismatch MWh (+41 %).
   - But it curtails 66.2 MWh under the correct model, against 52.3 MWh for binary
     substation feedback (B2). That is 1.27×, beyond the registered 1.1×, so H1(b) fails.
   - The same verdict holds in all five noise replications (1.23–1.27×; only the noise
     varies).
2. **Where the cost comes from** (post-hoc diagnosis):
   - **Under the correct model,** the cost is that of C1's registered safety margin s,
     which is part of the method. Without a residual to correct, C1 reduces to the
     same planner with the same margin (Rdev, 66.0 MWh). Without the margin, both reduce
     to plain replay (R0 and C1s0, 48.7 MWh).
   - Here s acts as a 2–4 percentage-point **loading** margin, because loading is what
     binds under the correct model: correct-model curtailment does not move under a
     ±0.005 p.u. busbar bias in 4 of 5 networks.
   - **Under model errors,** the same normalised s becomes a 0.002–0.004 p.u. **voltage**
     margin, and it costs 184 MWh (tap and MV). s was tuned where loading binds and was
     never tested as a voltage margin.
   - Acting on the errors itself costs curtailment: C1 curtails 829 MWh more than Rdev
     under model errors. Without the margin, C1s0 still curtails 15 % more than B3.
   - **Season (untested):** plain replay has 120 correct-model violations in the
     development half (spring) and 16 in the evaluation half (autumn). Per network-day,
     that is 0.16 against 0.017. In development, H1(b) **passed**: 91.0 against 86.7 MWh,
     1.05×. This is consistent with a seasonal cause; the mechanism was not isolated.
3. **The reliability gain is large and consistent across the 15 cells and 5 noise draws.**
   - C1 meets all **15 of 15** mismatch cells, against 9 for two exact meters, 12 for the
     Phase 6 persistence rule (B5) and 14 for exact full metering (B7).
   - Under the correct model it has 1 violation, against 48 for B2.
   - In development (learning phase) the gains were 166 against 1495 violations, with 9
     cells against 0.
   - It is **not** robust to a −0.005 p.u. busbar bias (260 violations, 11 cells; s. 5).
     All tested errors push in the favourable direction (s. 8).
4. **Mechanism.** The Phase 5–6 count feedback discarded continuous information that the
   substation measures. Substation-based estimation itself is known (Procopiou & Ochoa
   2017).
   - Under tap and MV errors, the busbar-voltage residual matches the true busbar offset
     to within 0.0001 p.u. in every network (v̂ +0.026 to +0.031). This was checked after
     the evaluation, evaluator-side (s. 11).
   - Because those errors shift all LV voltages almost alike, the correction also
     protects buses no sensor sees.
   - The transformer and feeder-head current ratios (+0.08 to +0.20) correct the PV
     reactive-power error.
   - In semiurb4 a mid-feeder cable is overloaded under the fixed-cos φ truth. B2 and B3
     leave 157 violations there, more than plain replay (R0, 111) or Rdev (102), because
     their τ sits at its floor. C1 leaves 0, by over-correcting: ρ_line 0.195, with 22.2
     against the oracle's 8.8 MWh.
5. **For the three constant, positive-direction truths tested, remote meters add almost
   nothing under the continuous method.** With 0, 1, 2, 4 or all meters (C1, C3),
   mismatch violations are 1, 0, 0, 0, 0, and mismatch curtailment rises from 1027 to
   1043, 1054, 1073 and 1133 MWh.
6. **Busbar voltage accuracy is the largest measurement dependency tested.** Per-customer
   injections were assumed exact.
   - Any busbar bias passes one-for-one into v̂.
   - **The tolerated bias is about the safety margin s.** In the networks with s = 0.002
     p.u. on voltage, C1 with a −0.002 bias equals C1s0 cell for cell under tap and MV.
   - With −0.002 p.u., mismatch violations are 17 (1 tap, 16 MV), with 15 cells.
   - With −0.005 p.u. (a class 0.5 device at its low-reading limit), they are 260, with
     11 cells. The rural margin of 0.004 p.u. is exceeded: rural2 has 33 tap and 14 MV
     violations.
   - At ±0.005 the bias equals the 0.005 label threshold, so the model-error label fires
     on 39–61 % of correct-model days in every network: a coin flip.
   - An LV busbar is usually measured directly, not through a voltage transformer. What
     matters is the accuracy of the whole measurement chain. Class limits are maximum
     permissible errors, not typical errors.
   - The pre-stated claim rule ("robust to busbar accuracy" only if H1 holds at −0.002)
     is **not met**, because H1(b) fails at every bias, as for C1 itself.
7. **Biased meters are mostly excluded, but the bias is not explained.**
   - Checked meter residuals keep the correct-model cost within 1 % of C1 in all five
     bias draws (at most 0.98 %). Under model errors, accepted positive biases still
     raise v̂: 1081 against 1027 mismatch MWh in draw 0.
   - Unchecked meters raise the correct-model cost to 83–146 MWh (H3 met).
   - The labels separate "meter bias suspected" (correct model) from "cannot tell bias
     from model error" (tap or MV). In draw 0 that is 732 of 915 days each. Over the five
     draws it is 732–915; in draw 0 semiurb5's bias went unflagged.
   - The split follows from the label definitions and the simulated truths. It encodes a
     prior; it does not identify the bias.

## 2. Methods compared (all with identical days, timing, actions and shared-sensor noise)

| ID | Policy |
|---|---|
| static | development-tuned static cap (open loop) |
| B2 | binary count feedback, substation, realistic readings |
| B3 | binary count feedback, + 2 topology meters, **exact** readings |
| B4, B5, B6 | binary feedback, + 2 meters, realistic: unfiltered, persistence on meters (Phase 6), guard band |
| B7 | binary feedback, every bus and line measured, exact readings |
| R0, Rdev | replay planner with no feedback: margin 0, development-tuned margin |
| **C1** | **residual-corrected replay, substation only, realistic readings, development-tuned margin** |
| C1x | as C1, exact readings |
| C1s0 | as C1, no safety margin (amendment 7A sensitivity) |
| C1lag | as C1, residuals used one day later (7A) |
| C3, C3x, C3_1, C3_4, C3full | as C1, with meter residuals checked against the busbar residual: 2 (realistic, exact), 1, 4 or all meters |
| C3b, C3nb | + 2 meters with per-meter bias (5 draws): checked, unchecked |
| C4 | hybrid: correction (no margin) plus the Phase 6 count feedback with persistence |
| oracle | the true grid, unattainable |

**C1 in one paragraph** (`margins.ResidualCorrector`).

- After each day, the busbar-voltage residual gives v̂: the measured value minus the
  nominal model's prediction for the same metered injections and applied caps, averaged
  over daylight. The transformer and feeder-head current ratios give ρ.
- The 7-day medians correct the replayed nominal exceedances: overvoltage + v̂,
  undervoltage − v̂, loading × (1 + ρ).
- The plan takes the least curtailment that keeps the corrected worst case of the past 28
  days below the limit, by a fixed safety margin s.
- s was tuned on development days from the nominal model alone: 0.04 for the rural
  networks and 0.02 for the others, normalised. That is 0.004 or 0.002 p.u. where voltage
  binds, and 4 or 2 percentage points where loading binds.
- Substation readings of day d act from day d + 1.

## 4. Observability and what the substation can see

**Violations at no action, by what could see them** (scored days, evaluator-side, + 2
topology placement):

| Truth | Substation-observable | At a metered bus | Unmetered overvoltage only | Unmeasured line only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| correct model | 1716 | — | — | 370 |
| tap −1 | 1588 | 3552 | 797 | 78 |
| MV 1.055 | 1472 | 4973 | 963 | 3 |
| fixed cos φ | 2639 | — | — | 355 |

**What follows.**

- **Binary substation feedback sees only the first column.** Remote voltage violations
  never reach it.
- **The continuous residual turns the busbar into a sensor of the common-mode error.** It
  covers the voltage columns for tap and MV errors without seeing any remote violation.
- **This works because these errors are common to the feeder.** A local error would not be
  seen. Examples:
  - a single customer's inverter;
  - a cable in poor condition;
  - a phase imbalance.
- **MV voltage varying within the day is common-mode but fast.** The busbar sees it every
  quarter-hour, but C1 uses a daylight mean, a 7-day median, and applies it the next day.
  Midday MV rises coincide with the PV peak, and an excursion of 0.01 p.u. exceeds s.
  Constant MV within the day is the most favourable case for C1.
- **Local errors.** Substation residuals alone are not expected to attribute them. This
  is argued, not tested: no local error was simulated. That two different local states
  give the same substation residuals holds to first order (they differ only through
  losses); it was not demonstrated.
- **Only the tested error directions are covered** (s. 8).

## 5. Measurement uncertainty, bias and identifiability

**Substation noise.**

- C1 against C1x (exact readings): 1 against 1 mismatch violations, 66.2 against 66.0
  MWh (H2 met).
- This is an implementation check, not evidence of robustness. After averaging, busbar
  noise (σ 0.002 p.u.) is about 0.0003 p.u., against offsets of about 0.03.

**Busbar voltage bias** (C1; amendment 7A):

| Busbar bias (p.u.) | Mismatch violations | Mismatch cells | Mismatch MWh | Correct-model MWh | Correct-model label rate, by network |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −0.005 | 260 | 11 | 725 | 66.2 | 0.41–0.61 |
| −0.002 | 17 | 15 | 897 | 66.2 | 0.0 |
| 0 | 1 | 15 | 1027 | 66.2 | 0.0 |
| +0.002 | 0 | 15 | 1154 | 66.4 | 0.0 |
| +0.005 | 0 | 15 | 1378 | 72.5 | 0.39–0.59 |

- **The bias moves the plan one-for-one.** A low-reading busbar under-corrects; a
  high-reading one over-curtails (+12 % mismatch MWh at +0.002).
- **The tolerated bias is about s.** Where s is 0.002 p.u., C1 at −0.002 equals C1s0
  cell for cell (for example urban6 MV, 11 violations and 118.6 MWh in both). Where s is
  0.004 p.u. (rural), −0.002 is absorbed and −0.005 is not.
- **At ±0.005 the mean v̂ is ±0.0050, exactly the 0.005 label threshold.** The label
  rates in the last column therefore sit on a knife edge (471 of 915 days at −0.005).
- **Two checked meters partly rescue a low-reading busbar:** C3 at −0.005 gives 88
  violations and 12 cells. But the labels assume a correct busbar: with the busbar at
  −0.005, the *unbiased* meters are labelled "meter bias suspected" on 278 correct-model
  days.
- **A busbar bias and a common-mode model error are not distinguishable in practice
  here.** A true offset leaves a small current signature (ρ ≈ −Δv/V: ρ_line −0.023 to
  −0.029 under tap and MV; ρ_trafo −0.027 to −0.029 under MV, 0 to −0.010 under tap); a
  busbar bias leaves none. To tell them apart at |b| ≤ 0.005 would need a current
  signature of at most about 0.5 % to be resolved, below the current-measurement error
  and the unknown voltage dependence of loads.
- **In this benchmark,** −0.002 p.u. raised mismatch violations from 1 to 17 with 15 of
  15 cells kept, +0.002 raised mismatch curtailment by 12 %, and −0.005 kept 11 cells. The
  robustness claim is not made, and no guidance for field equipment follows.

**Meter bias** (`biased_meters_matched`: random 0.002 plus a per-meter bias with σ 0.0098
p.u.; 5 draws):

| | Correct-model MWh per draw | Mismatch violations per draw | Mismatch MWh per draw |
|---|---|---|---|
| checked meters | 66.0–66.9 | 0–1 | 1025–1081 |
| unchecked meters | 82.8–145.6 | 0 | — |

- The check compares each meter's model residual with the busbar's model residual. It does
  not compare the voltages themselves.
- **The correct-model spread is busbar noise.** C3b in draw r equals C1 in replication r
  in 4 of 5 draws. H3 compares each draw with C1 replication 0, not with the matching
  replication; the verdict is the same either way.
- **H3 is tested only where voltage does not bind.** Under model errors, accepted positive
  meter biases raise v̂ through the `max` rule: C3b draw 0 curtails 1081 against C1's
  1027 MWh. The same `max` passes meter noise upward, which is why curtailment rises with
  the number of meters (C3x 1026.9 against C1x 1024.9 with exact meters).
- **Labels:**
  - Under the correct model, 732 of 915 days in draw 0 (732–915 over five draws) were
    labelled MEASUREMENT_MODEL_INCONSISTENCY (meter bias suspected).
  - Under tap and MV, the same disagreements were labelled
    INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION_TO_IDENTIFY_BIAS: bias and a local model error cannot be
    distinguished there.
  - That split holds by construction: the bias draws ignore the truth, and no truth
    contains a local error.
  - Under fixed cos φ, with exact and unbiased meters (C3x), the meter and busbar
    residuals disagree on 106 days and are labelled INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION_TO_IDENTIFY_
    BIAS. A distributed model error alone can make meters look inconsistent.
  - No bias is ever estimated or subtracted.

**Coverage of measurement intervals** (+ 2 meters, 5 draws; daily means over n = 20
midday readings):

| | Single reading ± 2u | Daily mean ± 2u/√n |
|---|---|---|
| independent noise | 0.954–0.956 | 0.945–0.961 |
| per-meter bias | 0.88–1.00 | **0.10–0.40** |

- Averaging intervals assume independent errors. A calibration bias does not average out,
  and an interval computed as if it did is wrong most of the time. The correct
  uncertainty of a mean is u = √(σ_b² + σ_r²/n).
- The values are nearly identical across truths (within 0.01), so there are about five
  independent values per row, not twenty.
- The single-reading coverage pools the meters. For one biased meter it is close to 0 or
  1.

## 6. Evidence labels (operational, not certificates)

| Truth | Label on C1 decisions (days, five networks) |
|---|---|
| correct model | EVIDENCE_SUFFICIENT 915 of 915 |
| tap, MV | PHYSICAL_MODEL_UNCERTAINTY 915 of 915 each |
| fixed cos φ | PHYSICAL_MODEL_UNCERTAINTY 890, EVIDENCE_SUFFICIENT 25 |

- **NO_VERIFIED_FEASIBLE_ACTION** never occurred. It takes precedence over every other
  label, so a day with both conditions would not count towards H5's model-error rate.
- **PHYSICAL_MODEL_UNCERTAINTY means "substation residuals beyond thresholds".** That is a
  model error *or* substation measurement bias. With a ±0.005 busbar bias, it fires on
  39–61 % of correct-model days in every network.
- **EVIDENCE_SUFFICIENT means only that the substation residuals are small.** It says
  nothing about local errors at unmetered buses.
- **There is no outcome for a biased reference.** The labels trust the busbar.
- **C3full** uses every line current in ρ_line, so its "substation residuals" label is not
  strictly substation-only. No hypothesis uses it.

## 8. What changes under each model error, and what is untested

- **Tap and MV (common-mode).**
  - The correction matches the true offset and protects unmetered buses.
  - Its cost is over-protection relative to the oracle: mainly the safety margin, then
    the replay worst case.
  - ρ of −0.023 to −0.029 relaxes loading planning. That is valid only for
    constant-power injections.
- **Fixed cos φ (distributed).**
  - The current ratios at the substation capture the extra loading. Here they over-
    correct the semiurb4 cable rather than match it (s. 3). That no cable is loaded
    beyond the worst feeder head's ratio was not shown in general.
  - The small negative v̂ (−0.004 to −0.007) relaxes voltage planning. That is safe in
    this direction: the reactive-power drop at remote buses is at least the busbar's, and
    the daylight mean is below the peak.
- **Untested** (amendment 7A item 10):
  - negative offsets (tap lowered, low MV), which would under-correct;
  - a reversed reactive-power error;
  - MV voltage varying within a day;
  - cables loaded beyond the head ratio;
  - voltage-dependent loads;
  - inverter Q(U) or P(U), and the inverter apparent-power limit (S = 1.053 S_n at full
    output and cos φ 0.95);
  - **errors in the customer injection data.** The residual is the reading minus the
    nominal model at the exact realised injections, so a systematic injection error
    passes one-for-one into ρ. A 5 % error equals the label threshold; the cos φ signal
    is 8–20 %. The binary policies' feedback signal does not depend on injection data.
- **Timing of the injection data.** A one-day delay changed almost nothing in the
  evaluation half (C1lag, 1027.07 against 1027.02 MWh). In development it changed 166 to
  220 violations. C1lag tests delay only, not error.

## 9. Scientific validity

**Exploratory, on examined data.** All results are an exploratory replication, with
these caveats:

- The criteria were set after a development probe, with Phase 5/6 known.
- H2, H4 and H5 pass by construction.
- The cells and networks are not independent replications.
- The noise replications vary only noise.

**Seasonal dependence (consistent with, not shown).** The evaluation half has falling PV:

- Plain replay with no margin has 16 correct-model violations in the evaluation half,
  against 120 in development. The halves differ in length (183 and 147 days); per
  network-day it is 0.017 against 0.16.
- H1(b) passed in development (1.05×) and failed in evaluation (1.27×).
- So the margin that development requires is a cost in evaluation. The mechanism was not
  tested, and a margin that stays adequate across seasons without that cost has not been
  shown.

**Can be claimed** (simulation, five benchmark networks, these truths):

- Substation residuals carry information about common-mode model errors and loading
  ratios that this project's count feedback discards.
- Using it removes almost all violations under three constant, positive-direction model
  errors without remote voltage meters, given exact next-day per-customer injections.
  Under those errors C1 curtails 41 % more than B3, and 15 % more without the margin
  (C1s0). Under the correct model its extra cost is that of its registered loading
  margin.

**Cannot be claimed:**

- that H1 holds;
- that C1 is cheaper than binary substation feedback;
- that the method works for local, negative-direction or time-varying model errors, with
  imperfect injection data, or on a real feeder;
- robustness to busbar accuracy, under the pre-stated rule;
- that the cost is seasonal (consistent, not tested);
- that any label certifies safety or identifies a bias.

## 12. Exploratory demonstrator (post-evaluation)

`python -m gridpulse demonstrate --network <code> --truth <truth> --output-dir <dir>`
writes one static HTML page (no scripts, no network access). The page:

- re-runs B2, B3 and C1 with the frozen code paths, and refuses to write if their
  violation or curtailment totals differ from the frozen evaluation outputs;
- picks the evaluation day with the most avoidable violations at no action, and its
  quarter-hour with the largest normalised exceedance. The rule uses the no-action state
  only, not the policies' outcomes;
- shows which quantities the operator measures, the true signed margins on the network
  diagram (p.u. and % never added), the caps per quarter-hour, C1's estimates and label,
  and the frozen half-year totals.

Ground truth appears for evaluation only. The page is labelled exploratory, synthetic
benchmark data. It is not a control system, a safety assessment or an operator tool.
