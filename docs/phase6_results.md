## 1. Answer in brief

1. **The Phase 5 noise defect is repaired, and repairing it does not change the Phase 5
   picture.**
   - With noise tied to each physical observation, unfiltered feedback with two meters
     still meets 14 of 15 mismatch cells and curtails 144 MWh under the correct model.
     Phase 5 reported 14 and 145.
   - The over-curtailment was real, not an artefact of the seeding defect.
2. **Under the correct grid model, and with meter noise that is independent per reading
   (the case persistence is designed for; amendment 6A), a simple persistence rule
   removes almost all noise-driven over-curtailment.**
   - Two meters: 144 → 55 MWh, against 53 MWh with exact readings (98 % of the excess
     removed).
   - Full metering: 308 → 69 MWh, against 54 (94 %).
   - Correct-model violations: 46 against 57 with exact readings, but against 31 with
     the unfiltered rule it replaces. For full metering: 11 against 25 exact and 2
     unfiltered.
3. **Under model errors, the rule's reliability, and the value of the meters, largely
   come from the noise.**
   - With two meters it meets 12 of 15 mismatch cells, *more* than exact readings (9).
     It does so by curtailing 57 % more (1144 against 728 MWh).
   - With exact readings the same rule meets only 4, the same as substation telemetry
     alone. With exact readings, persistence blinds the two meters.
   - Its mismatch cells rise with meter noise: 4, 9, 12, 14 for σ = 0, 0.005, 0.01,
     0.02.
   - With per-meter bias at the same total uncertainty, two meters meet 6–11 cells with
     382–4000 mismatch violations, depending on the bias draw. H2 fails in 4 of 5 draws.
     Full metering meets 15 in every draw, but it curtails 90–150 MWh under the correct
     model, against 69 MWh with independent noise and 54 MWh with exact readings. **The
     cost fix does not survive per-meter bias.**
   - **The registered H2 and H3 are met only because the meter noise is independent
     per reading.**
   - **Likely mechanism** (post-evaluation analysis):
     - with exact readings, the remaining violations are short and mostly at buses no
       sensor sees. Two meters, unfiltered: 393 of 513 mismatch violation steps (77 %)
       are flagged by no sensor, and 80 % of violation days have their longest run under
       45 min (76 % with the primary rule). A 45-min persistence rule cannot see them;
     - with noisy readings, three consecutive exceedances occur when a metered voltage
       stays within about σ/2 of the limit for hours, which the voltage-offset truths
       produce. These are **noise-induced near-limit alarms**: 152 alarm steps under
       model errors, 125 of them at steps without a true violation;
     - they track near-limit operation (real signal), but their strength is set by σ
       (uncalibrated).
   - So the rule controls cost, but it is **not** a noise-robust reliability mechanism.
     No rule tested here turns the exact-reading value of two meters (9 against 4 cells)
     into the same value under realistic noise without relying on that noise.
4. **In this benchmark, remote voltage readings add decision value. Under the primary
   rule, placement matters.**
   - **Registered (H3 met):** two meters met 12 mismatch cells against 4 for substation
     telemetry, at 55 against 52 MWh. Point 3 qualifies this.
   - **Information value without noise:** with exact readings and unfiltered feedback,
     two meters meet 9 against 4.
   - **Placement:** under the primary rule, deliberate placements of two meters met 11–14
     cells; random placements had a median of 7 (range 4–12; 19 of 20 met ≤ 10). The
     deliberate placements overlap:
     - topology = greedy detection in every network;
     - risk = topology in rural2 and semiurb4;
     - decision-aware = topology in rural2.
   - **Decision-aware placement was not better network by network** (H4 not met: 1 of
     5). Topology placement already met all three mismatch cells in 4 of 5 networks, so
     H4 could be won only in semiurb4.
     - Pooled: 14 against 12 cells.
     - At a cost of 85 MWh more curtailment over all truths (mismatch: 1228 against
       1144).
5. **Per-meter calibration bias makes the primary rule's outcome depend on the bias
   draw.**
   - At the same total uncertainty as the realistic condition: 6–11 mismatch cells over
     five bias draws, and 52–89 MWh under the correct model.
   - Persistence cannot filter a constant offset, which is established metrology
     (JCGM 100).
6. **The chi-square bad-data test detects a reactive-power imbalance, not "bad
   states".**
   - Under the PV reactive-power model error it flags 87–97 % of estimates. That is
     below the registered 90 % in rural2, because in late autumn there is too little PV
     to reveal the error.
   - Under the correct model it is badly calibrated (1.5–37 % false flags) and catches
     71 % of gross estimation errors. H6: (a), (b) and (c) are all not met.

## 3. Why noisy feedback over-curtails (development diagnostics and post-evaluation)

- **True (simulated) violations are smaller than the meter noise.**
  - At no action on development days, under the correct model, voltage violations exceed
    the limit by a median of 0.001–0.0035 p.u. (70–94 % below 0.005 p.u.).
  - Under MV 1.055 the median is 0.007–0.014 p.u.
  - Specifications bound smart-meter voltage error at about σ ≤ 0.008 p.u.:
    - the VDE FNN requirement, 2 % at the 99th percentile, gives σ ≤ 0.008–0.009;
    - a ±1 % specification read as a 2–3σ limit gives σ ≈ 0.003–0.005.
  - σ = 0.01, independent per reading, is therefore pessimistic. (Corrected after the
    post-evaluation review; the protocol's "consistent with practice" overstated this.)
  - Field data show real overvoltage events lasting about 5 min (Kreuwel et al. 2021).
    That is a shorter time scale than the 15-min steps simulated here, and 15-min
    averaging meters would dilute such events.
  - A single reading cannot separate most real violations from noise.
- **Count-based online calibration reacts to every false 0→1 flip.**
  - The margin rises until the *measured* violation count falls to β = 0.1 per day.
  - With noise, that means pushing every metered voltage well below the limit.
  - This is the mechanism analysed in general form by Wang, Zecchin & Simeone (2026).
- **In closed loop, the remaining violations are short and often unmetered**
  (post-evaluation diagnostic, `docs/provenance/phase6/scripts/post_evaluation_runs.py`).
  - Exact readings, two meters, unfiltered: 166 of 207 violation days (80 %) have their
    longest violation run under 3 quarter-hours.
  - The same with the primary rule: 217 of 287 (76 %).
  - With exact unfiltered readings, 77 % of mismatch violation steps are seen by no
    sensor.
  - A rule that waits for persistence misses them unless noisy near-limit readings form
    runs. That happens selectively: under the correct model the primary rule curtails
    at most +0.007 MWh/day more than exact readings in any network. Under tap and MV
    errors it curtails +0.11 to +0.50 MWh/day more, except in semiurb4 (0).

## 7. State estimation: what the chi-square test can and cannot detect

- **Detects a reactive-power imbalance.**
  - With substation readings the test has 2 degrees of freedom: transformer P and Q
    against the summed injections. The busbar voltage is used up by the MV-voltage state.
    J is in effect a feeder power-balance test.
  - It flags most estimates under the PV reactive-power error: 87–97 % per network
    (evaluation). Pooled by month under that truth: July 0.99, December 0.78.
  - rural2 has the least PV: 0.97–1.00 July–September, then 0.90 in October, 0.79 in
    November and 0.51 in December. H6(a) failed there (0.87). That is consistent with
    too little PV in November and December to reveal the reactive-power error.
- **Cannot see voltage offsets.**
  - A tap or MV-voltage error is absorbed by the estimated MV voltage.
  - J and flag counts under those truths match the correct model (642, 642 and 638 flags
    for nominal, tap and MV). The gross-error counts are similar (174, 161, 153).
  - The busbar-voltage consistency check (the Phase 5 MODEL_INCONSISTENCY label) does
    see the offset.
- **Is not calibrated under the correct model.**
  - False flags on good estimates are 1.5–37 % by network, against a nominal 1 %.
  - The implied J is about 1.1× (semiurb4) to 4.7× (rural3) larger than the test
    assumes, and the false flags rise in December.
  - Hypothesis, not tested: the pseudo-measurement σ (forecast-error spread)
    understates partly correlated and biased forecast errors.
- **Misses gross errors that look plausible.**
  - Under the correct model, 174 sampled estimates were grossly wrong:
    - 158 by line loading only (more than 20 points);
    - 16 by voltage (more than 0.02 p.u., all in semiurb5).
  - They cluster on a few days and in urban6 (95 of 174).
  - The test caught 71 % in total, and 7–100 % by network. At 0.01 p.u. / 10 points it
    caught 41 %.
  - Hypothesis: the test sees only the summed injection error. Errors redistributed
    between feeders, while the total stays near zero, are invisible to it, and feeder-head
    currents are not estimator inputs (amendment 5A).
- **Conclusion.** A converged WLS estimate with an accepted chi-square test is **not**
  evidence of a correct state here. The test is useful as a model-consistency signal for
  reactive power, which the Phase 5 consistency label already provides. A reliable
  detector of wrong states was not developed from the available information.

## 9. Scientific validity: what can and cannot be claimed

**Can be claimed** (on five PV-stressed SimBench LV networks, 2016 profiles, these four
model truths, simulation only):

- Phase 5's noise-driven over-curtailment is real and survives the seeding repair.
- Under the correct model, and for independent per-reading noise, a 45-min persistence
  rule on meter voltages removes most of it, with no more violations than exact readings
  (though more than the unfiltered rule: 46 vs 31).
- Two remote readings met 12 of 15 mismatch cells against 4 for substation telemetry.
  Deliberate placements beat random ones.
- Calibration bias and short closed-loop violations limit what the tested
  reading-level filters (single-reading guard band, persistence) can do.

**Cannot be claimed:**

- that persistence makes the controller noise-robust; its mismatch reliability depends
  on the noise level;
- anything about real meters, which average and carry systematic error;
- that guard bands fail in general (only a single-reading 2u guard was tested);
- that the chi-square test detects model errors in general;
- statistical significance, or independence of cells, replications or networks;
- confirmatory evidence, operational readiness, or anything about a real utility.
