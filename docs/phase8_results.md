**Identity.**

- The three stress truths and four of the five customer-data conditions (I1–I4) are

- No independent LV benchmark exists here. **Nothing is confirmatory, and nothing is

**Reading guide.** As in Phase 7:

- Totals are over the five networks other than rural1.
- "Mismatch" covers the tap −1, MV 1.055 and PV fixed cos φ 0.95 truths.
- A *cell* is (network, truth). The requirement β is at most 0.1 avoidable violation

## 1. Answer in brief

1. **H1 is NOT met.** Fixed separate reserves (C5) did not reach the registered 10 %
   curtailment reduction in this benchmark.
   - C5 keeps C1's reliability: 1 against 1 mismatch violation, 15 against 15 cells, so
     non-inferiority holds.
   - It saves 6.3 % under the correct model (62.0 against 66.2 MWh), but only 2.6 %
     under model errors (1000.4 against 1027.0 MWh).
   - **The saving is not attributable to the separation here.**
     - C1f, one shared margin on the same fine grid, saves the same 6.3 % under the
       correct model and more under model errors (972.0 MWh, 5.4 %).
     - C5's loading reserve came out identical to C1f's in every network.
     - So C5 against C1f tests one thing only: the registered rule that tunes r_V on a
       one-tap-step shift. With that rule, separate reserves cost 2.9 % more than the
       fine shared margin. Other r_V rules were not tested.
2. **H2 is NOT met.** Rolling reserves (C6) curtail less but give up the strict
   reliability margin.
   - **Evaluation half:** C6 curtails 16 % less under model errors (863.2 MWh) and 20 %
     less under the correct model (52.7 MWh), and meets the requirement in all 20
     cells. But it has 29 mismatch and 13 correct-model violations against C1's 1 and 1,
     beyond the +15 and +5 margins. This holds in every noise replication (28–33
     against 0–1).
   - **Development half:** C6 was non-inferior to C1, exactly at the long-runs margin.
     It had fewer violations (159 against 166; 42 against 56) and more cells (12
     against 9). But it cost 12 % **more** under the correct model.
   - **Both halves together** (descriptive, post-hoc, with C1's tuning half in-sample):
     −8.5 % mismatch MWh and −1.8 % correct-model MWh, with 188 against 167 and 55
     against 57 violations.
   - Under the protocol this is a **trade-off within the requirement**. C1s0 and C5L
     earn the same label in the evaluation half. C6 differs from them only in the
     development half, where it met 12 cells against their 0 and 3.
3. **Customer data errors (H3).**
   - **C1 and C5 met the registered robustness rule** under meter noise (I1), unmetered
     load reactive power (I2), PV recorded 5 % high (I3) and 10 % missing data with a
     week-old fallback (I4).
   - **Neither met it under a two-day data delay (I5).** All cells are still met, but
     correct-model curtailment rises by 36 % (C1) and 31 % (C5), through re-tuned
     (larger) margins.
   - **C6 met the rule under the delay**, although its violations rose. It failed under
     missing data on curtailment alone (1.11×, just over the 1.1 limit).
   - **B3 met the rule under none of them**: it meets only 9 of 15 cells with exact data.
4. **Substation instrumentation (H4).**
   - Noise ×2.5, a two-day delay and 20 % missing days met the rule for C1, C5 and C6.
   - **A busbar bias of −0.005 p.u. fails the rule for all three** (260, 1603 and 2631
     mismatch violations), the most of any tested condition.
   - At −0.002 p.u., C1 and C5 met the rule (17 and 27 violations). C6 did not (301
     violations, 10 cells).
   - The tested high-reading biases (+0.002 and +0.005) met the rule and raised
     curtailment (C1 1.09× at +0.005, just under the limit).
5. **Stress truths (H5).** The predictions written before the physics was computed
   held:
   - **Opposite-direction tap:** every corrected policy covers it (5 of 5 networks).
   - **MV voltage rising with PV:** no attainable policy covers it (2–3 of 5).
   - **A wrong cable in the records:** no attainable policy covers it (3 of 5). The error
     is in the cable's recorded **thermal rating**, not in the flows. In semiurb4 every
     attainable policy has exactly the no-action count of violations (878).
6. **Margins, observed.**
   - The voltage reserve matters under common-mode errors: without it (C5L) mismatch
     violations rise from 1 to 33, although C5L still met the requirement in all 15
     cells.
   - **Post-hoc, one cell (rural3, mv_daily).** More total reserve meant fewer
     violations (C1 and C1f 52, C5 215, C5L 302, C1s0 415), partly through the loading
     reserve. None met the requirement.
   - **In this benchmark, most of C1's excess over the oracle is attributed by
     subtraction to the replay itself, not the margin.** Under model errors, C1 curtails
     458 MWh more than the oracle:

     | Source | MWh | Comparison |
     |---|---|---|
     | Coarse grid | 55 | C1 − C1f |
     | Fine margin | 137 | C1f − C1s0 |
     | The 28-day replay | 266 | C1s0 − oracle |

**What the two halves show.** This is one benchmark year, so it is a contrast between
two halves, not a seasonal law.

- **Fixed reserves tuned on the spring half leave spring cells failing.** C1, C1f and C5

- **In autumn they cut violations far below β.** C1s0 already meets β in all 15
  mismatch cells, and the margin removes 38 of its 40 violations.
- **C6's reserves move with the half-year.**
  - In autumn they fall to about zero: per network, mean r_V ≤ 0.0005 p.u. and r_L ≤

## 4. Separation against resolution (H1 attribution)

**Under the correct model voltage does not bind** in this benchmark's 0.90–1.10 p.u.
band. A tighter planning band would change this. C5 and C1f then curtail the same MWh in
every network (62.0 MWh in total), because C5's r_L equals 100 × C1f's s everywhere.

**Under model errors the voltage reserves differ:**

| Network | C5 r_V (p.u.) | C1f voltage (0.1·s, p.u.) | Mismatch MWh, C5 | Mismatch MWh, C1f |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rural2 | 0.003 | 0.0025 | 153.8 | 140.1 |
| rural3 | 0.0015 | 0.004 | 370.6 | 391.1 |
| semiurb4 | 0.0015 | 0.0015 | 53.9 | 53.9 |
| semiurb5 | 0.002 | 0.001 | 157.5 | 140.0 |
| urban6 | 0.0025 | 0.002 | 264.5 | 246.9 |

- **The tap-step rule gave a larger voltage reserve than the shared one in three
  networks**, which only costs curtailment under constant errors.
- **In rural3 it gave a smaller one** (0.0015 against 0.004 p.u.). That is the source of
  C5's saving there, and of its rural3 failures:
  - under mv_daily (215 violations);
  - under the −0.005 busbar bias (1233 of C5's 1603 violations come from rural3 mv_high).
- **What was manipulated, and what was not:**
  - Only the grid resolution was varied in isolation: C1 → C1f is −5.4 % (mismatch) and
    −6.3 % (correct model).
  - The other cost sources named in s. 1 are interpretations of the decomposition; they
    were not isolated.
- **H1's attribution label "none"** is the code's label for "H1 not met". It is not a
  measured zero effect of separation.

## 5. Rolling reserves (H2)

| Condition | Result | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Non-inferiority, evaluation half | 29 against 1 mismatch violations; 13 against 1 correct-model violations | **no** |
| Non-inferiority, development half | holds (long runs 13 against 12 + 1, exactly at the margin) | yes |
| Cost, evaluation half | 0.84× (mismatch) and 0.80× (correct model) of C1 | yes |

- **A structural property** (the novelty review's point). C6 calibrates against the
  operator's own corrected replay, so its target can never contain the model error or a
  busbar bias. Its reserves fall to about zero in autumn, and a busbar bias never raises
  them: mean r_V stays at 0.0000–0.0008 p.u. under −0.002.
- **Prior art.** Adaptive constraint tightening calibrated on *observed* violation
  frequencies already exists (Oldewurtel et al. 2013, 2015; Korda et al. 2014; Gibbs &
  Candès 2021; this project's Phase 4 online margin). C6 differs from it only in what it
  calibrates against.

**Post-hoc observation** (not registered; exploratory; evaluation half). Against the
Phase 7 baselines:

- C6 curtails within 1 % of binary substation feedback under the correct model (52.7
  against 52.3 MWh, 1.007×). It has 13 against 48 violations there, and 29 against 513
  under model errors against B3.
- **C1s0 meets both conditions too** (48.7 MWh; 40 violations).
- Under the correct model, the open-loop static cap dominates B2, B3 and C6.
- In the development half, C6 curtails 17 % more than B2 under the correct model (101.6
  against 86.7 MWh).
- Whether these differences between the halves are seasonal is untested.

- **I3 (PV recorded 5 % high) is traded, not absorbed.**

- **Not causal under I5, S3 and S4.** Two recorded diagnostics,

- **A low-reading busbar produced the most violations of any tested condition.** The
  tolerated low bias follows the voltage reserve in force:
  - In four networks C1's reserve is at least C5's.
  - In urban6, where C5's is larger (0.0025 against 0.002), C5 was hurt less: 20

  - The replay's spread still absorbs some bias (C6 goes from 29 to 301 violations, not
    to thousands, at −0.002).
  - C6's self-calibration cannot see the bias at all (s. 5).

  mean and the daily median, but bias is not. −0.005 p.u. corresponds to about a class
  0.5 measurement chain.

## 8. Stress truths (H5)

"Covered" means at least 4 of 5 networks meet the requirement. "Created" counts avoidable
no-action violations absent under the nominal model. Both stress cases are stylised.

| Truth | Created (per network) | Covered by | Not covered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| tap_lower_lv (LV −2.5 %) | 4–62 | static, C1, C1s0, C1f, C5, C5L, C6 (5/5); B2, B3 (4/5) | — |
| mv_daily (MV + 0.03 × the network's PV share; peak ≈ +0.018 p.u.) | rural3 310, urban6 81, semiurb5 2, others 0 | — | every attainable policy (2–3 of 5) |
| line_local (one cable about two sizes smaller: R × 1.5, rating × 0.8, X unchanged) | semiurb4 399, urban6 319, others 0 | — | every attainable policy (3 of 5) |

**Opposite-direction tap.** Two separate facts:

- Relaxing overvoltage planning is safe because the true voltage really is lower (v̂ −0.025
  to −0.026 p.u.).
- The harm is extra current, which the feeder-head ratio captures (ρ̂_line +0.026 to
  +0.028, close to 1/0.975 − 1). Every created violation is a loading violation.
- The binary policies' semiurb4 failure is not caused by the tap. They already fail there
  under the correct model (B2 30, B3 35).
- This reasoning assumes constant-power injections; voltage-dependent loads are not
  modelled.

**Time-varying MV voltage.**

- The busbar sees the swing every quarter-hour. The limit is the estimator: a
  daylight-mean, next-day offset (v̂ ≈ +0.005 p.u. against a midday peak of +0.018).
- The two remote meters (B3) catch most of it in urban6 (5 violations against C1's 242),
  but not in rural3 (122).
- Rural3 is the post-hoc cell of s. 1 item 6. In urban6 the spread across corrected
  policies is small (232–258).
- A constant offset cannot track a time-varying disturbance (the internal model
  principle, Francis & Wonham 1976), so this failure is expected, not new.

**Wrong cable.** The error is in the recorded rating, not in the flows.

- The measured currents match the model: ρ̂_line ≈ 0 in semiurb4 and urban6.
- The extra resistance creates no voltage violation (rural3: 0 created).
- No tested measurement or policy revealed it. In urban6 one B3 meter lies downstream of
  the cable, yet B3 still fails.
- A current meter compared against the *recorded* rating would plausibly not reveal it
  either, because the current is about the modelled one. Whether a voltage residual could
  detect the R × 1.5 change was not tested.
- A corrected asset record, or thermal monitoring of that cable, is the hypothesised
  remedy (not tested).

**Predictions** (protocol s. 9, written before any physics was computed):

- tap_lower covered: **held**.
- mv_daily not covered by substation-residual policies: **held**.
- line_local not covered by substation-only policies where violations are created:
  **held**.
- **"B3 covers line_local only where a meter lies downstream":**
  - It is not contradicted, because B3 covers it nowhere.
  - Its implicit premise, that a downstream voltage meter would see the error, is
    refuted: the error is thermal.
  - Amendment 8A (written while the physics ran, after reviewers had inspected some
    cached statistics) redefined only how violations are counted as "created". It
    changed no prediction.

## 10. What is known, what was built, what was found

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| Existing knowledge | Per-constraint tightening (Roald & Andersson 2018; tube and stochastic MPC). Adaptive tightening from observed violation frequencies (Oldewurtel et al. 2013, 2015; Korda et al. 2014; Gibbs & Candès 2021). Offset-free correction (Muske & Badgwell 2002), and that a bias cannot be told apart from a disturbance (Pannocchia & Rawlings 2003). The internal model principle (Francis & Wonham 1976). The price of robustness (Bertsimas & Sim 2004). Metering-error effects (Muscas et al. 2014; Alimardani et al. 2015). Robust operating envelopes (Liu & Braslavsky 2024) |
| Engineering implementation | Separate p.u. and % reserves on the Phase 7 planner. A rolling per-type reserve calibrated on the operator's own corrected model. Physics for erroneous metered injections (plan and residual reference alike). Three stylised stress truths. A guarded staged study with digests of the physics cache |
| Experimental findings (simulation, exploratory, this benchmark) | H1 and H2 not met. With the registered tap-step rule, separation added nothing beyond a finer shared margin. Rolling reserves curtailed 16–20 % less in the evaluation half, at more violations within β, and cost more under the correct model in the development half. The tested consistent metering errors (I1–I3) met the robustness rule, but I3 was traded, not absorbed. A two-day delay raised fixed-reserve curtailment by about a third. A −0.005 p.u. busbar bias produced the most violations. The time-varying MV and wrong-cable cases were not covered by any attainable policy, as predicted |
| Possible contribution | A controlled ablation on five SimBench LV networks over one year: it separates margin structure (one r_V rule), resolution (a resolution-matched control) and adaptivity (one calibration target and window), and maps the failures of one substation-residual planner over data and instrumentation conditions. **No new method** |
| Unverified hypotheses | A time-resolved correction (for example an offset regressed on PV level) would cover MV swings; it could also absorb PV-correlated reactive-power errors. A corrected asset record or thermal monitoring would close the wrong-cable gap. Adaptive reserves calibrated on partly observed violations, with a floor from a bound on the busbar error. Any of this transferring to real, unbalanced feeders |

## 11. Post-evaluation reviews

Four AI-assisted reviews were run after the evaluation:
- A + B, physics and measurement;
- C + D, decision theory and statistics;
- E, software and reproducibility;
- F, novelty and claims.

Each finding was checked against the outputs before it was applied.

**Verdicts and integrity.**

- **Verdicts (C + D):** every hypothesis verdict recomputes as stored (H1, H2, 20 H3, 21
  H4 and 30 H5 cover decisions). About 300 numbers were checked; the mismatches are
